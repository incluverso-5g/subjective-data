# Debate Study with Local and Remote Participation

## Overview
The test consisted of implementing a debate activity in a school setting. Two teams of four students were formed, and participants were informed in advance of the debate topics and whether they were required to argue in favor of or against each topic.

For remote participants, an “owl” device was provided, allowing one remote participant per team to teleport into the debate environment. A total of eight students took part, debating four different topics, ensuring that all participants experienced both local and remote participation modalities.

---

## Pre-Questionnaire
1. **ID**: Unique identifier for each participant.
2. **User ID**: User identifier (alias or code).
3. **Age**.
4. **Gender**: Male / Female.
5. **Vision**: Normal or corrected.
6. **Nationality**.
7. **Experience level using VR headsets**: Basic, Intermediate, etc.
8. **Debate experience level**: Basic, Intermediate, Expert.

---

## Post-Questionnaire
Four debates were conducted during the test, and the topic of each debate was recorded.

Each debate involved two roles:
- One team arguing **in favor** of the agreed topic.
- One team arguing **against** the agreed topic.

It was also recorded whether participants used VR headsets, indicating whether they attended the debate as **travelers (remote)** or **locals**.

### Measures
1. **Aggregate Quality of Experience (QoE)** — 5-point scale
2. **Social Presence**
   - LSPQ (locals → remote avatars on tablet)
   - RSPQ (remote → locals on video)
   - ASPQ (remote → remote avatars)
3. **Spatial Presence (PPQ)** — remote users only
4. **Liking** — 7-point scale
5. **Self–Other Overlap (IOS)**
6. **Emotional State (I-PANAS-SF)**
   - Positive Affect (PA)
   - Negative Affect (NA)
7. **Open-ended handwritten responses**

---

## Participants
Eight participants (ages 13–15, M = 14.13, SD = 0.6) took part in the study, including three females and five males from different nationalities. Seven participants reported an intermediate level of experience with XR technology.
